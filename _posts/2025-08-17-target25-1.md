**D2. Not-so-simple Mail Protocol**

**Challenge**
>We've received a menacing extortion email from an unknown sender, claiming that sensitive data has been stolen from the company. The email demands a ransom and threatens to release the data if the demand is not met within 48 hours.<br>
>Unfortunately, it's clear based on the extortion email that the sender attempted to send this email before... Since the demand's due date is relative, we need to find the first email sent so we can estimate a lower bound on the deadline for the legal and finance teams to plan around.<br>
>Can you trace the digital breadcrumbs and find the first extortion email sent by this threat actor?<br>

**How I found the flag**
1. Opened Opensearch 
2. Changeed the Index Pattern to "SMTP" because I wanted to view email servers and other related information.<br>
*What is SMTP?* 
>Email client uses SMTP to connect to your email provider's mail server and send the message. The server then uses SMTP to forward the email to the recipient's mail server, which ultimately delivers the email to the recipient's inbox.
3. Set the date range to what I wanted to view.
4. Searched for “security@personalyz.io”, which was the security team's email address.
![Opensearch1](/assets/img/target2025/target2025_d2_1.png)
5. There were many emails, over 300, but I remembered that the subject the threat actor used in the email contained "data".
![Opensearch2](/assets/img/target2025/target2025_d2_2.png)
6. Search for "data" using the search function (Ctrl+F).
7. Found an email! I double-checked that the email path matched. Yes, it used 250.24.46.164.
If I hadn’t found the email by searching for "data," I could have also used 250.24.46.164 to search for related emails.
![The result](/assets/img/target2025/target2025_d2_3.png)

**MITER ATT&CK Mapping**
>**Tactic**: Impact [TA0040](https://attack.mitre.org/tactics/TA0040/)  
>This challenge falls under the Impact tactic in the MITRE ATT&CK framework. In this case, the attacker is attempting to persuade the organization by threatening to release stolen data.
>  
>**Technique**: Financial Theft [T1657](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1657/)  
>The attacker demands a payment in Bitcoin in exchange for not leaking sensitive data. This aligns with T1657: Financial Theft, where adversaries steal or demand money directly from victims. While this technique often involves unauthorized financial transactions, blackmail for cryptocurrency also fits within its scope.
